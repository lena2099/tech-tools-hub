# Chapter 1

The first thing Chen Lin became aware of was the smell. Hay, sweat, and something metallic—blood, probably—all mixed with the unmistakable tang of cheap ale that had been left out too long. The second thing was the voice in his head.

> [System boot sequence initiated...]
> [Welcome, Debugger Unit #001. N.E.X.U.S online.]
> [Your current physiological state: Critical. Recommend immediate medical attention.]

*Great,* Chen thought, *I died from overwork and got isekai'd into a tavern brawl.* He tried to open his eyes, but one of them was swollen shut. The other revealed a low wooden ceiling, rough-hewn beams, and the concerned face of a woman who looked like she'd seen one too many bar fights to be truly alarmed.

"You alive, stranger?" she asked, wiping a rag that was more red than white.

"Define 'alive,'" Chen croaked. His throat felt like sandpaper. "Did I win?"

The woman snorted. "You took a bottle to the head from a man twice your size. Then you headbutted him in the groin and passed out. I'd call it a draw."

> [Combat Log Retrieved: You sustained 47 damage from a blunt instrument (improvised weapon).]
> [You dealt 23 damage via critical hit to the groin area. Target incapacitated.]
> [Skill Gained: Improvised Combat (Level 1).]

A skill for nutshots. Chen's previous life as a developer had not prepared him for this particular career path, but he supposed transferable skills were overrated anyway.

"Where am I?" he asked, slowly sitting up. The room spun, then stabilized as his system—*his* system, apparently—adjusted something in his inner ear.

> [Equilibrium Correction Applied. Balance restored to baseline.]

"The Rusty Cog, Thornwall," the woman said. "You stumbled in three hours ago, babbling about 'syntax errors' and asking for the 'lead developer.' We thought you were a Null Sect spy. They're the only ones who talk nonsense like that."

Chen's heart—which he was pretty sure had stopped beating in his original world—skipped a beat. Null Sect. The cult that worshipped runtime errors. In the game he'd spent the last three years of his life developing, they'd been a minor faction, a joke reference to bugs in the code. But here, in this too-real world with its too-tangible pain, they were apparently a threat.

"I'm not with the Null Sect," he said quickly. "I'm... a Debugger. It's a different kind of crazy."

The woman—the bartender, he realized, from the apron and the weary expression—raised an eyebrow. "A what now?"

> [System Notification: You have encountered an unregistered class designation. Recommend discretion.]
> [Current Reputation with Thornwall Local Faction: Neutral (-3).]

*Noted,* Chen thought. Aloud, he said, "It's a long story. Do you have any water? And maybe a mirror?"

The bartender handed him a chipped ceramic cup filled with water that tasted faintly of copper. Chen drank it greedily, then caught his reflection in the window behind her.

He looked... terrible. Gaunt, with dark circles under his eyes that reminded him uncomfortably of his deathbed. But it was *his* face. The same sharp jaw, the same perpetually tired expression. He hadn't just been dropped into someone else's body—he'd been dropped into *himself*, somehow remade in this world's image.

> [Character Sheet Available. Display?]

*Yes.*

A translucent blue screen appeared in his peripheral vision, visible only to him. It was the familiar interface from his game, but cleaner, more organic. Less like a UI and more like a living document.

> **Name:** Chen Lin
> **Level:** 1
> **Class:** Debugger (Hidden/Unique)
> **HP:** 13/60 | **MP:** 45/45 | **Stamina:** 22/50
>
> **Attributes:**
> STR: 8 (-2 penalty from malnutrition)
> DEX: 9
> INT: 16 (+2 bonus from class)
> WIS: 12
> CHA: 7
> LUK: ???
>
> **Abilities:**
> Debug Mode (Active) - View and modify underlying code of objects, spells, and entities.
> Syntax Highlighting (Passive) - Identify errors and inconsistencies in magical and physical systems.
> Breakpoint (Active) - Temporarily halt a process or spell. Cooldown: 24 hours.
>
> **Equipment:**
> Tattered Commoner's Tunic (Armor: 0)
> Worn Leather Belt
> Empty Coin Purse
> ??? (Unknown Item in Inventory)

Chen blinked at the last line. He didn't remember having anything in his inventory. He focused on it, and a small leather-bound notebook materialized in his hand.

> [Item Unlocked: Developer's Journal]
> [A personal notebook from your previous life. Contains notes on the original codebase of Aethoria. Fragile.]

His hands trembled as he opened it. The pages were filled with his own handwriting—notes, code snippets, architecture diagrams. The last entry was dated the day he'd died: *"Found it. The bug isn't in the game. It's in the compiler itself. Someone inserted a backdoor. If I can trace it, I can—"*

The entry cut off. Below it, in a different hand—neat, sterile, digital—was a single line:

> *"Stop looking, Debugger. Or I'll delete you from the stack."*

Chen closed the notebook. His heart was pounding now, and it wasn't from the head wound. The Admin knew he was here. The Admin had *always* known.

"You look like you've seen a ghost," the bartender said.

"Something like that," Chen muttered. He stood, wobbling slightly, and grabbed the edge of the bar for support. "I need to get to the Guild Assembly. Where's the nearest chapter house?"

"Thornwall doesn't have one. Nearest is Bridgewatch, three days east." She studied him with narrowed eyes. "But you won't make it five miles in your condition. And even if you did, the Guild won't register a man without coin or reputation."

> [Quest Available: "Prove Your Worth"]
> [Objective: Increase reputation with Thornwall local faction to Friendly (25).]
> [Reward: 100 XP, Basic Adventurer's Kit, Map of the Eastern Reaches.]
> [Failure Condition: Death or expulsion from Thornwall.]

"Fine," Chen said. "What needs doing around here that nobody else wants to touch?"

The bartender—Marta, according to the nameplate Chen hadn't noticed before—gave him a long, appraising look. "There's a rat problem in the old mill. But it's not normal rats. They've been glowing."

*Of course they have,* Chen thought. *Because nothing in this world can just be simple.*

> [Quest Accepted: "Glowing Rats of the Old Mill"]
> [Recommended Level: 2]
> [Warning: Anomalous magical signature detected. Proceed with caution.]

"Where's the mill?" he asked.

"East end of town. Can't miss it—it's the one with the green glow." Marta handed him a rusted short sword from under the bar. "Here. You'll need this more than I will."

> [Item Received: Rusted Short Sword (Damage: 4-8, Durability: 12/20, Quality: Poor)]

"Thanks," Chen said, strapping it to his belt. It felt awkward, unnatural. He was a developer, not a fighter. But desperate times, et cetera.

As he walked toward the door, Marta called out, "One more thing, stranger. The rats only come out at night. And it's already dusk."

Chen paused at the threshold. Outside, the sky was a bruised purple, the first stars beginning to pierce the twilight. In the distance, he could see a faint green luminescence pulsing from the old mill like a diseased heartbeat.

> [System Warning: Night cycle approaching. Monster spawn rate increased by 25%.]
> [Debug Mode available. Use wisely.]

He stepped out into the cooling air, the notebook burning a hole in his mental inventory. Somewhere out there, the Admin was watching. And somewhere in the code of this world, there was a backdoor that could either take him home or get him killed.

*Just like old times,* he thought grimly. *Another deadline, another bug to fix. Except this time, the bug might be me.*

The green light pulsed. And Chen Lin, former full-stack developer and current Debugger, walked toward his first quest with nothing but a rusted sword, a cryptic notebook, and a voice in his head that might be his only ally—or his greatest enemy.

> [N.E.X.U.S: "For the record, I recommend against this course of action. Your survival probability is 8.3%."]

*Noted,* Chen thought. *But what's the fun in playing it safe?*

> [N.E.X.U.S: "Fun. Yes. I will add that to my understanding of human irrationality."]

The mill loomed ahead, its green glow casting unsettling shadows. And somewhere in the darkness, something squeaked—a sound that was too high-pitched, too synchronized, too *wrong*.

Chen tightened his grip on the sword and stepped through the door.
