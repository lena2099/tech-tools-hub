# Best Fitness Tracker Under $100 in 2026

I've lost two Fitbits to washing machines. Not because they weren't waterproof—because I forgot I was wearing them. So when I went shopping for a replacement this spring, I had one rule: it had to be cheap enough that I wouldn't cry when I inevitably destroyed it, but smart enough to actually track my runs.

Here's what I learned after burning through a few return boxes.

## What Actually Matters (And What Doesn't)

First, ignore the step count wars. Every tracker under $100 is within 5% accuracy of each other. What actually separates the good from the junk is the app experience and battery life. A tracker with a terrible app is a paperweight—you'll stop syncing it by week two.

Second, check if the screen is visible in sunlight. I bought one last year that I literally couldn't read at noon on a running trail. That's useless. Look for transflective displays or high-nits AMOLED panels.

Third, don't pay extra for GPS unless you run without your phone. If you carry your phone (like 90% of us), connected GPS is fine.

## The Three Worth Your Money

### Xiaomi Smart Band 9

This is the one I actually wear daily. It's $45, and it does 90% of what a $300 smartwatch does. The AMOLED screen is bright, the battery lasts two weeks, and the band weighs nothing.

The Mi Fitness app is surprisingly clean—no ads, no subscription to unlock basic sleep data. It tracks HR, SpO2, sleep stages, and has 150+ workout modes. I use the swim tracking almost every weekend.

**Con:** The band is small. If you have wrists bigger than 7 inches, it looks like a toy. Also, the quick-release strap mechanism is finicky—I've popped it off twice just catching it on a jacket sleeve.

### Fitbit Inspire 3

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Fitbit+Inspire+3&tag=technolo0b423-20)

This is the one I wanted to love. It's sleek, the sleep tracking is genuinely the best in this price range, and the Fitbit app is still the gold standard for long-term trends. The battery lasts 10 days, which is solid.

I bought this one first. Returned it after a week. The screen is dim—I struggled to read it outside, and the touch response is laggy. It also doesn't have a dedicated button, so navigating back is a swipe dance that gets old fast.

**Con:** You need a Fitbit Premium subscription ($10/month) to see your sleep score breakdown and readiness score. The tracker works without it, but it feels intentionally crippled. That's annoying for a $99 device.

### Amazfit Band 7

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Amazfit+Band+7&tag=technolo0b423-20)

This is the budget king at $35. It has a decent AMOLED screen, 18-day battery life, and it tracks all the basics. The Zepp app is... fine. Not great, not terrible. It gets the job done.

I kept this as a backup for travel because I don't care if it gets stolen or lost. It's that disposable. But if you want detailed sleep stages or accurate VO2 max estimates, this isn't it. The HR sensor drifts during interval workouts—it'll read 140 when you're actually at 165.

**Con:** No built-in GPS (connected only), and the vibration motor is weak. I slept through two alarms with this thing. That never happened with the Xiaomi.

## FAQ

**Do I need a subscription for any of these?**

Only the Fitbit Inspire 3 pushes Premium for advanced metrics. The Xiaomi and Amazfit are completely free—no paywalls for sleep or HR data.

**Will these work with iPhone and Android?**

All three work with both. The Xiaomi app is slightly better on Android, but it's fine on iOS. None of them have the seamless pairing of an Apple Watch, but you'll get notifications and call alerts just fine.
