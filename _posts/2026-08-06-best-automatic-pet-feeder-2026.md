Everyone says you need a smart pet feeder that connects to your phone, has a camera, and dispenses treats on command. That's marketing fluff. The real problem is way simpler: you need food to come out reliably, every single time, without jamming or going rogue. Most feeders fail at exactly that.

I've spent six months running four different automatic feeders with my cat Mochi, and I've read more Reddit threads about jammed kibble and dead apps than I care to admit. Mochi is a grazer, so I needed tiny, accurate portions dispensed throughout the day. That's where most feeders completely fall apart. They either dump too much, jam on the third day, or the app forgets the schedule entirely.

The popular advice says buy the cheapest feeder with good reviews. That's bad advice. I bought one of those — a $40 unit with a flimsy flap door. It jammed on day four, and the backup battery died in a week. Don't do that. The real answer is spending more upfront to avoid a hangry pet and a wasted $40.
